How to Sell a Car You Still Owe Money On

Selling a financed car is completely normal — most trade-ins and plenty of private sales involve an existing loan. The dealer or buyer pays off your remaining balance directly to the lienholder as part of the transaction; you get whatever's left over if the car is worth more than you owe, or you cover the difference if it isn't.

The Basics

Payoff, Lienholder & Equity

Your payoff amount is what you actually owe

Your loan payoff isn't the same as your remaining monthly payments added up — it's a specific figure your lender quotes, usually valid for a set number of days, that includes any accrued interest. Get a current payoff quote directly from your lender before you negotiate a sale price, so you know exactly what needs to be covered.

The lienholder gets paid first, automatically

Whoever holds the lien on your title — your bank or finance company — has to be paid off before the title can transfer cleanly to a buyer. In a dealer trade-in or a dealer-network sale, this is handled directly between the buyer and your lender as part of closing; you're not expected to personally coordinate a payoff wire.

Positive equity vs. being underwater

If the car is worth more than your payoff amount, that difference is yours — either as cash or as credit toward your next purchase. If you owe more than it's worth (being "underwater" or "upside down"), you'll need to cover that gap out of pocket, roll it into a new loan, or wait until you've paid down enough to have equity again. Knowing which situation you're in before you start the process avoids an unpleasant surprise at closing.

In Practice

What This Looks Like When You Sell

Nothing about having a loan on the car should stop you from getting real offers on it — just disclose it upfront. A buyer or dealer who does this regularly will simply factor your payoff into the numbers and handle the lienholder directly at closing.
