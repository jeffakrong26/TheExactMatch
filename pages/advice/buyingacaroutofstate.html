Buying a Car Out of State: What Actually Changes

Buying from an out-of-state dealer is common and entirely normal — the wider your search, the better the odds of finding your exact spec at the right price. What changes is mostly paperwork, not risk: titling goes through your home state either way, sales tax is generally paid where you register the car (not where you bought it), and a pre-purchase inspection matters more, not less, since you can't just walk the lot in person as easily.

Paperwork

Titling & Registration

Where you register is what matters

The car gets titled and registered in your home state, regardless of where the dealer is located — that part doesn't change. Most dealers selling out of state are used to this and can handle out-of-state paperwork correctly, but it's worth confirming directly before you commit: ask specifically how they handle out-of-state title work and whether they've done it before.

Sales tax is usually based on where you register, not where you buy

Most states tax a vehicle purchase where it's registered, not where it was sold — so you typically won't pay sales tax twice. Exact rules, rates, and any reciprocity agreements between specific states vary and change, so confirm the current requirement with your home state's DMV or tax authority before you buy, not after.

Temporary tags for the drive home

If you're driving the car home yourself rather than having it shipped, the selling dealer typically issues a temporary transit tag valid for the trip. Confirm its validity window covers your actual drive time, especially for a longer haul.

Getting It Home

Inspection & Shipping

A pre-purchase inspection matters more here, not less

You can't kick the tires in person as easily on an out-of-state car, so a third-party pre-purchase inspection (PPI) — someone local to the car, not affiliated with the seller — is worth the cost every time. Ask for a full photo/video walkaround too, including cold-start video and any panel-gap or paint-meter readings if it's a higher-value vehicle.

Shipping vs. flying out to drive it home

Enclosed or open-carrier transport is generally the simpler option for longer distances, and cost scales with distance and vehicle size. Flying out and driving it back yourself costs your own time and adds mileage before you've even taken ownership at home, but some buyers prefer it for the experience or to inspect the car in person before paperwork is final. Either way, get shipping quotes in writing before you commit to the purchase, not after.
