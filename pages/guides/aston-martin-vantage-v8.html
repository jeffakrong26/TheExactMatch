The 2019+ Aston Martin Vantage V8: A Real Buyer's Guide.

The third-generation Vantage started around $200,000 new and now trades in the $90,000s — a genuine exotic badge, a Mercedes-AMG-sourced twin-turbo V8, and modern styling and tech, for less than a new base 911 costs today. Below is what actually matters before you buy one: the specs, current market pricing, the known issues owners report, the maintenance you should budget for, and what to look for on a specific car — from a team that's sourced one for a client.

Why It Makes Sense Right Now

One of the smartest buys in today's market.

Look at what actually exists in the $90,000–$100,000 range right now: the options thin out fast. Narrow for genuinely modern tech and styling, and the field shrinks again. Narrow again for real performance — a twin-turbo V8 with a sub-4-second 0–60 — and you're left with very few cars. Add a genuine exotic badge on top of that, and the Vantage stands close to alone.

Context matters here. This car started around $200,000 new. It dropped into the $80,000s at its low point and has settled into the $90,000s today — while a new base Porsche 911 now runs $134,650+, a Lotus Emira starts at $109,400, and a Corvette Z06 starts near $130,000. You're getting into a car that once cost double what its modern rivals cost new, for less than any of them costs today.

Worth watching: earlier Vantage generations (roughly 2008–2015) have started climbing too, but that means older infotainment and older-generation dynamics — clean examples from that era run around $60,000, versus the $90,000s for a 2019+ car with modern tech and styling.

Is It Reliable?

The honest answer.

Aston Martin as a brand hasn't had a reputation for the strongest reliability — some of that comes from the broader (and not entirely fair) notion that British cars in general are less dependable. For the third-generation V8 Vantage specifically, a few recurring issues show up in owner reports:

  • A whining rear differential at highway speeds.
  • Coil pack corrosion that can lead to misfiring.
  • Touchy brake calibration at low speeds.
  • Build quality that occasionally falls short of expectations at this price point.

None of these are dealbreakers on their own, but they're worth knowing before you buy. There was also a recall on 2019–2020 model year Vantages related to the airbags — this has been addressed, but confirm any car you're considering has had that recall work completed.

Scheduled Maintenance

What to budget for.

  • 10,000 mi / 1 yr — 131-point factory inspection, tire rotation, oil and filter change.
  • 20,000 mi / 2 yrs — brake and clutch fluid replacement, tire rotation, cabin filter, synthetic engine oil.
  • 30,000 mi / 3 yrs — another 131-point inspection, drive belt replacement, engine oil and filter.

A routine oil change runs $250–$500 depending on independent shop versus dealer. Minor service can run up to $1,500. Major service intervals can run $2,500 or more depending on what's needed.

Before You Buy

What to look for.

  • The black exterior pack — which blacks out the trim — is the most sought-after single option on these cars.
  • Other options worth prioritizing — the carbon fiber pack, carbon-ceramic brakes, the carbon fiber and Alcantara sports steering wheel, upgraded wheels, and Q by Aston Martin paint. On the rarer end, the Dynamic Pulse and Lunar Eclipse designer packages are genuinely special-order combinations worth seeking out.
  • Where it's being sold — a large share of used Vantages sit at non-Aston Martin dealerships. A pre-purchase inspection is strongly recommended on any car you're considering — or better, buy from an authorized Aston Martin retailer directly.
  • Recall status — confirm the 2019–2020 airbag recall work has been completed.
  • CPO coverage — a car bought through an official Aston Martin dealer has already been through the manufacturer's full 130-point inspection before listing, and typically includes a 1-year CPO warranty. Depending on how long you plan to keep the car, that's the difference between real peace of mind and an expensive surprise down the road.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is the Aston Martin Vantage reliable?

Aston Martin isn't known as the most reliable brand overall, and the 3rd-gen V8 Vantage has some recurring owner-reported issues — rear differential whine, coil pack corrosion, brake calibration at low speeds. None are dealbreakers, but a pre-purchase inspection is strongly recommended before buying.

How much does it cost to maintain a Vantage?

Routine oil changes run $250–$500. Minor services can run up to $1,500, and major service intervals can run $2,500 or more, depending on what's needed.

What's the best year to buy?

A 2019–2021 example currently offers the best value — the same core platform and performance as later years, at a meaningfully lower price than 2022+ cars.

Should I buy from an Aston Martin dealer or an independent seller?

Buying from an authorized Aston Martin retailer — or getting an independent pre-purchase inspection if buying elsewhere — is strongly recommended. Factory-inspected cars often come with CPO warranty coverage that can save significant money down the line.
