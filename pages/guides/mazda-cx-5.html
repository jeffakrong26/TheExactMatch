The 2026 Mazda CX-5: A Real Buyer's Guide.

The CX-5 is all-new for 2026. It's bigger inside, all-wheel drive is standard on every trim, and it starts at $31,485 with destination. The turbo engine is gone, though, and a lightly used previous-generation CX-5 now costs thousands less. Below: the specs, what each trim costs, how the new car compares with a used one, what dealers are offering, and what it costs to own.

Specs At A Glance

The numbers that matter.

ENGINE 2.5L four-cylinder POWER 187 hp TORQUE 186 lb-ft DRIVE AWD standard EPA 24 / 30 / 26 mpg
  • Transmission: 6-speed automatic. Most rivals use eight speeds or a CVT.
  • Space: the wheelbase grew about 4.5 inches. Rear legroom is 39.9 inches, and cargo space is 33.7 cu ft behind the rear seats and 66.5 cu ft folded, up from 29.1 and 59.3.
  • Tech: Google built-in infotainment, with a 12.9-inch touchscreen on lower trims and a larger screen on Premium Plus.
  • What's coming: Mazda has said a hybrid is due for the 2027 model year.

Specs from Mazda and Kelley Blue Book. EPA figures are combined across trims.

Who It's For

The honest pitch.

The CX-5 is Mazda's best-selling vehicle in the US, and it has long been the compact SUV for people who care how a car drives and how its cabin feels. The new one adds the rear-seat and cargo room the old one was short on, and its starting price undercuts the Honda CR-V and Toyota RAV4.

It's not the pick for everyone. Reviewers measured 0–60 at around 9.6 seconds, slow for the segment, and there's no hybrid until 2027. If fuel economy is the priority, a CR-V or RAV4 hybrid is the better buy today. If you want power, a used previous-generation CX-5 Turbo has a lot more of it.

Every 2026 CX-5 uses the same engine and all-wheel drive, so the trim decision is about equipment. Destination is $1,495 and non-standard paint adds $595. The base price is up $940 over the 2025 model.

New Or Used?

The previous generation is the real competition.

The second-generation CX-5 (2017–2025) is plentiful on the used market, and recent model years cost well under a new base car. It also offered something the new one doesn't: a 2.5L turbo with 256 hp on premium fuel.

How Dealers Price It

Incentives and what to push on.

Mazda has put money on the hood of the new CX-5 since launch. Through September 2026, published offers included up to $1,750 in customer cash, or 0% APR for 36 months with a $750 financing bonus, depending on region and trim. Some dealers also advertised 0% for 60 months on approved credit and a $500 military bonus. Cash and special financing usually can't be combined, so compare the total cost of each.

  • Start from the incentive, not the sticker. Know the current national and regional offers for your exact trim before you talk numbers.
  • Ask for the out-the-door price in writing, itemized. Dealer add-ons (protection packages, nitrogen tires, etching) and market adjustments show up there.
  • Get competing quotes. Every trim uses the same powertrain, so the same trim in the same color is directly comparable between dealers.

Incentives change monthly and vary by region. These are September 2026 offers. Check current offers before you buy.

Ownership Costs

What to budget for.

The naturally aspirated 2.5L is the simplest engine Mazda offers, with no turbo or hybrid system to maintain. Kelley Blue Book estimates about $5,200–$7,300 in maintenance over five years for recent non-turbo CX-5s, plus roughly $1,900–$2,000 in repairs over the same period. Expect the 2026 model to land in a similar range once KBB publishes figures for it.

KBB five-year cost-to-own estimates for 2024–2025 2.5 S models. Actual costs vary by mileage and region.

Questions

Straight answers.

How much does the 2026 Mazda CX-5 cost?

It starts at $31,485 for the 2.5 S, including the $1,495 destination charge, and tops out at $40,485 for the 2.5 S Premium Plus. Every trim has the same 187-hp engine and standard all-wheel drive.

Does the 2026 CX-5 have a turbo?

No. The new generation launched with only the 2.5L four-cylinder (187 hp). Mazda has said a hybrid is coming for the 2027 model year. If you want the turbo, look at a used 2019–2025 CX-5 Turbo.

Should I buy a new CX-5 or a used one?

A used 2023–2025 CX-5 averages roughly $26,000–$29,000, several thousand under a new base model, and the Turbo is available used. The new car is roomier, has newer tech and a full warranty. If rear-seat and cargo space matter, the new car earns its premium.

Are there incentives on the 2026 CX-5?

Yes. Through September 2026, Mazda offered up to $1,750 in customer cash or 0% APR financing with a bonus, depending on region and trim. Offers change monthly, so check current incentives before you negotiate.
